\chapter*{Abstract}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{Abstract}
\thispagestyle{plain}

\begin{english}
USB peripherals are among the most widely exploitable physical-access attack
vectors. A device that claims to be a flash drive can also present itself to the
operating system as a keyboard and type commands within milliseconds (BadUSB
attacks). On Linux, the kernel binds a driver to every new device as soon as it
appears, without asking the user.

This thesis presents the design, implementation and evaluation of
\textsf{Probolos}, a deny-by-default USB admission gate for Linux. Probolos uses
the kernel's USB authorization interface (\texttt{authorized\_default}) so that
every new device arrives \emph{switched off}. While the device waits, it is
examined in up to four stages: (a) identity, from its descriptors; (b)
consistency, with 21 rules that detect contradictory claims such as a storage
device that also declares a keyboard; (c) behaviour, by briefly enabling the
device with its input exclusively grabbed (\texttt{EVIOCGRAB}) and analysing
keystroke timing; and (d) storage, by reading the partition table read-only,
without mounting. The final decision always belongs to a human, through the
terminal or desktop dialogs whose friction scales with risk. The system keeps a
device history with descriptor fingerprints to detect descriptor drift, applies
privilege separation between a small root gate and an unprivileged analyzer,
and guarantees that the gate reopens on every exit path so that the user is
never locked out.

The evaluation used emulated USB devices (dummy\_hcd and raw\_gadget), a suite of
1413 automated tests, property-based testing, coverage-guided fuzzing and
repeated code audits. 106 defects were logged and fixed, four of them of the
highest priority (P0). The thesis concludes that a deny-by-default gate combined
with consistency checks substantially reduces the BadUSB attack surface at a low
usability cost, and documents the limits of the approach.

\vspace{0.8cm}
\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} USB security, BadUSB, keystroke injection, Linux,
sysfs, device authorization, privilege separation, peripheral security, anomaly
detection.
\end{english}
